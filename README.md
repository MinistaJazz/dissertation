# Dissertation: Afrotemporalism and the AI Family

This repository contains the doctoral dissertation of Rev. Jasmaine Acelia Cook Kendrick, M.Msc., submitted to the University of Sedona in May 2026.

**Title:** *Afrotemporalism and the AI Family: Diasporic Intelligence for Pastoral Counseling*

## What is in this repository

- `dissertation.md` — the final submission file.
- `ai_detector_genre_baseline_receipt.md` — Sammaritano (2019) statistical comparison establishing the Sedona / IMM genre baseline for contemporary AI detection.
- `LICENSE` — Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International.

## What is not in this repository

This public repository contains the submission file and the Sammaritano comparison receipt. The dissertation references "the repository" as the audit trail for prior drafts (v1–v4), basement-cast role-disclosure statements, the IMM transcript, the SVDT Declaration of Trust, Voice Bible v3, Consent Risk Index v1.0, and other supporting receipts. Those artifacts live in a private audit repository under Sisterhood Voice Data Trust governance, available for committee or scholarly verification on documented request via ministajazz@wearemuchdifferent.com.

The public repository does not duplicate the audit trail because consent-governance is what the dissertation argues for. The methodology is published; the governance is held.

This pattern follows standard scholarly practice for sensitive supporting evidence: IRB-protected interview transcripts, qualitative field notes, Indigenous-methodology source materials, and IP-bearing technical artifacts are routinely referenced in published scholarship and made "available on reasonable request" rather than uploaded to public repositories.

## on what the cuts taught me

the submission version of this dissertation runs at the 25,000-word ceiling Sedona requires. the first draft ran longer. to fit, i cut about a thousand words. what surprised me was what the cuts revealed.

i cut a section on Babel and Pentecost that pre-emptively answered a Christian theological objection most readers would never raise. i cut a section called *Theology of the Individual Held in a Collective Covenant* that pre-emptively answered the academic-left critique that my work is Silicon Valley libertarianism in a robe. i cut a section called *Likely Objections and My Responses* that answered four objections i had already answered elsewhere in the dissertation, more powerfully, the first time.

when i read the cuts back, the pattern was clear. i was defending the work before it had been challenged. i was defending my lineage, my cognitive disability, my AAVE, my church, my method, my title, my receipts. twice. three times. sometimes four.

two scholars name this pattern.

Claude Steele (Stanford, 1995) calls it **stereotype threat**: the cognitive load of having to disconfirm a stereotype the world is already projecting onto you. you over-perform, over-cite, over-credential to preempt the misread. the labor of carrying that defensive layer has measured cognitive cost. for a writer with a 91/16 cognitive split, that layer eats the exact resource i needed to write.

Sherman James (epidemiologist, 1983) calls it **John Henryism**: the high-effort coping pattern of working twice as hard to be heard half as much. named for the folk hero who outworked the steam drill and died of the exertion. James originally documented it in Black men's cardiovascular outcomes. later work extended it to Black women's pattern of doing the receipts work before anyone has asked, in every room.

stereotype threat names the cognitive load. John Henryism names the cumulative cost.

the dissertation was carrying both.

the defensive sections were not weakness. they were survival writing under a four-hundred-year stereotype the academy has never disconfirmed. but survival writing belongs in the journal, not the body of a doctoral submission. the body holds the argument and trusts the host to greet the reader.

so i cut what i could. the cuts are not lost. they are now THE REPORT essays, repository receipts, basement archive. each section gets to be itself in the room it belongs to.

if you are a disabled Black woman writing scholarship inside an institution that was not built for you, this is the noticing i want to leave on record. the defending is real. and you do not have to do it alone.

~ Jazz
